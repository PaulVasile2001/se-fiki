01 / THE WHY · M1

Good ideas shouldn't
get stuck alone.

Starting something new can be difficult when you have an idea but lack a co-founder, technical skills, or access to the right people.

Startup Helper aims to make it easier for aspiring founders and people with useful skills to discover each other and explore working together.

01↗

THE PROBLEM

Finding the right people is hard.

People may have promising startup ideas but struggle to find collaborators with complementary technical knowledge, experience, or motivation.

02✳

OUR SOLUTION

A place to connect.

Startup Helper is envisioned as a platform where people can share startup ideas, present their skills, and find potential collaborators.

03◎

VALUE PROPOSITION

From idea to collaboration.

Help idea owners and skilled contributors discover relevant people and take the first step towards building something together.

MILESTONE 02 · CUSTOMER DISCOVERY

Validation 1
(The Idea)

1. Problem & Initial Solution

How we identified the problem: As university students, we noticed that many peers have great ideas but lack the technical skills to build them. Conversely, tech-savvy students often look for cool side projects but lack a concrete idea or business plan.

What we thought is a solution: A matching platform where people can create profiles, share ideas, and find collaborators with complementary skills.

2. Customer Discovery Plan

To validate this, we needed to test our core assumptions without pitching our product. Based on the Mom Test, we designed our interview guide to focus on past behavior and actual evidence rather than future intentions.

Key Assumption tested: People actively try to find co-founders/collaborators but fail because existing platforms (like LinkedIn or Facebook groups) are not built for early-stage idea matching.

3. Execution & Findings

Team effort: Paul Vasile, Poenaru Octavian, and Bogdan Rosetty reached out to 2 customer segments: Aspiring Founders (Business/Management students) and Technical Builders (Computer Science students).

How we reached them: Face-to-face exploratory interviews on campus and a targeted Google Form distributed on specific student WhatsApp/Discord groups.

The Questions We Asked

  • "Tell me about the last time you had a startup idea but couldn't execute it. What did you do?"
  • "How did you try to find a technical partner for that specific project?"
  • "What was the hardest part about working with someone you just met on a project?"
  • "How much time/money have you spent trying to solve this collaboration problem in the past?"

Our Findings:

  • Founders: 70% tried asking friends first, and when that failed, they abandoned the idea. They fear idea theft when posting on public forums.
  • Builders: They are hesitant to join "idea guys" unless there is a clear scope of work and mutual commitment. Trust is the biggest barrier.
[!] Add your Google Forms / Interview Notes screenshots or links here as proof.

4. Impact on Our Product

These insights directly affected our solution. We realized Startup Helper cannot just be a "Tinder for startups."

Pivot/Changes: We are adding trust-building features to our platform: NDA templates, verified skill badges, and mandatory "Project Scope" fields so technical builders know exactly what is expected of them before they commit.

5. Ready for Customer Validation?

Yes. We consider ourselves ready to move to the Customer Validation stage (testing selling/MVP). The problem is highly validated: people are experiencing this pain point, they have taken actual steps to try and solve it (posting in mismatched groups, attending networking events), and our modified solution directly addresses their trust and matching issues.
