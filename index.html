01 / THE WHY · M1

Good ideas shouldn't
get stuck alone.

Starting something new can be difficult when you have an idea but lack a co-founder, technical skills, or access to the right people.

Startup Helper aims to make it easier for aspiring founders and people with useful skills to discover each other and explore working together.

01↗

THE PROBLEM

Finding the right people is hard.

People may have promising startup ideas but struggle to find collaborators with complementary technical knowledge, experience, or motivation.

02✳

OUR SOLUTION

A place to connect.

Startup Helper is envisioned as a platform where people can share startup ideas, present their skills, and find potential collaborators.

03◎

VALUE PROPOSITION

From idea to collaboration.

Help idea owners and skilled contributors discover relevant people and take the first step towards building something together.

MILESTONE 02 · CUSTOMER DISCOVERY

Validation 1
(The Idea)

1. Problem & Initial Solution

Our starting problem hypothesis: Someone with an early-stage idea may not know where to find collaborators with the skills needed to move it forward. At the same time, people with useful skills may be interested in joining a project but lack a clear way to discover suitable opportunities. This came from our observations as students; it is a hypothesis, not yet a proven or widespread problem.

Initial solution concept: A platform where people describe their skills, interests, and availability, while idea owners outline a project's goals and needs. Users could discover relevant profiles or projects and start a conversation. We have not established yet whether people need a dedicated platform, what information would make a match useful, or whether existing channels already work well enough.

2. Customer Discovery Plan

Our goal is to learn about people's real experiences before presenting the platform concept: whether they have recently looked for a collaborator or project, what they tried, and what happened. We want to speak with both idea owners and people with complementary skills, and keep the two perspectives distinct when reviewing the answers.

Key assumption to test: People in both groups have a recurring difficulty finding a suitable match and have tried specific workarounds that leave the need unresolved. The first interview also surfaced a separate possible need around finding startup guidance and funding opportunities; we will investigate that rather than assume it belongs in the matching product.

The first interview was exploratory. Some questions asked about intentions and preferences rather than a specific past event, so the next interviews will put more emphasis on concrete examples and actions already taken.

3. Execution & Findings

Team effort and current status: Paul Vasile, Poenaru Octavian, and Bogdan Rosetty are working on customer discovery with two intended perspectives: people with startup ideas and people with skills who might join a project.

Planned questions for upcoming interviews

These are prompts for the conversations, and may vary based on the responses received.

  • "Have you thought about starting your own startup? What was your latest idea?"
  • "How have you thought about validating that idea?"
  • "Tell me about the last time you tried to find a collaborator for a specific idea or project. What did you do?"
  • "Where did you look, and what happened?"
  • "What part of starting or joining a startup would be easier if it were automated?"
  • "What was difficult about the approach you tried, if anything?"
  • "For people looking to join a project: tell me about the last time you looked for one. How did you choose what to pursue?"

4. Impact on Our Product

What the early evidence suggests: The form responses describe different startup ideas and early validation approaches. In the recorded interview, Teodor described a business-consulting idea and said that finding relevant funding opportunities could be automated. He also expressed interest in joining a startup whose purpose aligned with his own idea. This points to possible needs around guidance and opportunity discovery, but it does not show that he has tried and failed to find a co-founder or that a matching platform would solve that problem.

Product impact so far: We are not claiming a pivot or adding features based on this small sample. We are keeping collaborator matching as a hypothesis and sharpening the next round of discovery: test it separately with idea owners and potential contributors, and check whether funding or startup guidance is a distinct need. There is not yet evidence to prioritize features such as verified profiles or NDA templates.

5. Progressing Toward Customer Validation

Current stage: We are building an initial understanding of the people we want to serve and the challenges they may face. The four form responses and exploratory interview have given us useful starting points, including different approaches to developing and validating ideas, interest in joining a startup, and a possible need for help finding relevant funding opportunities. These early signals help us decide what to explore next; they are not yet enough to confirm which need should shape the product.

Next step: Continue the planned interviews with people who have startup ideas and people who may want to contribute their skills. We will focus on specific experiences—what they needed, where they looked, what they tried, and what was still missing—and compare the patterns across both perspectives. We can then refine the product concept and prepare a simple prototype or matching-flow walkthrough to gather feedback before making decisions about an MVP. This is a normal part of the customer-discovery process: learning early gives us a stronger basis for building something people may genuinely find useful.
